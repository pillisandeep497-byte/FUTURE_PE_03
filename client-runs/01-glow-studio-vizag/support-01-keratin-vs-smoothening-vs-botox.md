## The short answer

**Keratin** smooths frizz and keeps some volume and movement. **Smoothening** straightens your hair flatter and holds it longer. **Hair botox** repairs damage and doesn't straighten at all. If your hair is frizzy but you still want body, choose keratin. If you want it genuinely straight, choose smoothening. If your hair is damaged from previous treatments, repair comes first — botox, not straightening. *(verify pricing with studio)*

Here's who each one is for, without the sales pitch.

## Keratin: for frizz you want to control, not eliminate

Keratin coats the hair cuticle in protein and seals it with heat. The result is hair that dries faster, frizzes far less, and still falls naturally — you keep your movement.

**Choose it if:** your hair is frizzy and dry, takes forever to dry, or turns unmanageable in coastal humidity, and you don't want it to look flat or ironed.

**Think again if:** your hair has been bleached repeatedly in the last few months, or you want genuinely straight hair — keratin won't deliver that, and you'll be disappointed by week three.

In Visakhapatnam's humidity, keratin is the most requested of the three, because it solves the specific problem people have here: hair that behaves in the morning and doesn't by lunch.

## Smoothening: for hair you want genuinely straight

Smoothening uses a different chemical process that relaxes the hair's natural pattern, producing a flatter, sleeker finish that lasts longer than keratin — typically 4–6 months.

**Choose it if:** your hair is thick, heavy and wavy, you're tired of the volume, and you want a straight, low-maintenance finish.

**Think again if:** your hair is fine or thinning (smoothening removes the volume you need) or you like movement and body in your hair — smoothening makes hair noticeably flatter, and it grows out with a visible line if you don't maintain it.

It's also the treatment people most often regret choosing too quickly, because it removes exactly the thing they liked about their hair. If you're unsure between keratin and smoothening, start with keratin — you can always go straighter later, but you can't easily add volume back.

---

## Hair botox: repair, not straightening

Despite the name, hair botox contains no botulinum toxin and it does not straighten hair or freeze it in place. It's a deep repair treatment that fills in damaged areas of the hair shaft with proteins and amino acids, leaving hair stronger, softer and less prone to breaking.

**Choose it if:** your hair is damaged from bleaching, colouring or an old straightening treatment; it feels rough, snaps easily, or has lost its shine.

**Think again if:** you want straight or significantly smoother hair. Botox won't do that, and if a stylist promises it will, ask them to explain exactly how — the honest answer is that it won't.

**One useful detail:** hair botox is often the right *first* step. If your hair is too damaged for keratin, we'll recommend botox or a protein repair, then reassess in 6–8 weeks. It's a smaller bill now and a better result later.

## How a professional actually decides

There's no single "your hair type" answer. Four things decide it:

1. **Hair history.** What you've done in the last 6–12 months matters more than what your hair looks like today. Bleach and repeated colour change the plan entirely.
2. **Porosity and elasticity.** How quickly hair absorbs and holds moisture, and how far it stretches before snapping. This is checked strand by strand, and it's the reason a strand test exists.
3. **What you actually want.** "Smooth" and "straight" are different requests. Sort that out before choosing a treatment, not after.
4. **How much maintenance you'll do.** Someone who washes daily and swims weekly will get less from keratin than someone who washes twice a week. Any stylist who doesn't ask about your routine is guessing.

If a studio recommends a treatment within five minutes of you walking in — without checking your hair or asking about its history — that recommendation isn't about your hair.

## What if you choose wrong?

Nobody's hair is ruined by a correctly executed treatment of the wrong type — the most common outcome is simply disappointment.

- **Keratin when you wanted straight:** hair is smoother but still has movement. Not a disaster; upgrade to smoothening next time.
- **Smoothening when you wanted volume:** hair is flatter than you like. It grows out; plan on regular trims and a shorter interval between touch-ups.
- **Botox when you wanted smooth:** hair is healthier and shinier but frizz remains. Pair it with keratin once the repair has taken.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is keratin the same as smoothening?**
No. Keratin smooths and reduces frizz while keeping volume and movement. Smoothening relaxes the hair's pattern for a flatter, straighter result that usually lasts longer.

**Which lasts longer, keratin or smoothening?**
Smoothening generally lasts a little longer — around 4–6 months versus 3–5 months for keratin — though aftercare changes both.

**Does hair botox straighten hair?**
No. Hair botox is a repair treatment. It adds protein, softness and shine; it does not straighten or smooth hair like keratin does.

**Can I get keratin and colour in the same visit?**
Sometimes, depending on your hair's condition and the order of services. A colour service also requires a patch test at least 24 hours beforehand. Ask during the assessment — it's a decision made on your hair, not a standard rule.

**Which is best for curly hair?**
It depends what you want to keep. Keratin loosens curls while preserving some pattern; smoothening removes most of it. If you love your curl pattern, keratin is usually the safer choice.
