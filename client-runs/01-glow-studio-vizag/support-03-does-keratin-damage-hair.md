## The direct answer

A professionally applied keratin treatment on suitable hair does not damage it — and for many people it makes hair easier to manage and less prone to breaking from daily heat styling. Damage happens in three specific situations: hair that was already over-processed gets treated anyway, the clarifying wash or the sealing step gets skipped, or the treatment is repeated too often. All three are avoidable, and all three are decisions a salon makes, not a fault in the treatment itself.

If your hair has been bleached twice in the last three months, we will tell you no and suggest a repair plan instead. That's not a sales tactic — keratin on already-compromised hair is where the horror stories come from. *(verify that this remains studio policy)*

## What actually goes wrong (and why it isn't "keratin")

Almost every "keratin ruined my hair" story traces back to one of these:

**1. The clarifying wash was skipped.**
Keratin needs a clean cuticle to bond to. Product buildup, oil and residue block it. Applied over buildup, the formula sits on the surface, the hair feels coated and stiff, and it fails within weeks. It's the single most common shortcut, and it's invisible to the customer.

**2. The sealing step was rushed or incomplete.**
The smoothness people pay for comes from flat-ironing in thin sections at controlled temperatures. It takes 40–60 minutes. Rushed sealing produces hair that's coated but not sealed — which feels dry and can look worse than before.

**3. Hair that shouldn't have been treated, was.**
Over-bleached or chemically compromised hair has already lost structural integrity. Adding further processing — any further processing — risks breakage. A responsible studio checks for this and refuses the booking.

**4. The treatment was repeated too frequently.**
Most professional guidance suggests keratin no more than about three times a year. Stacking treatments every few weeks compounds chemical exposure on the same hair.

Notice what isn't on that list: the word keratin. The treatment isn't the variable. The process and the assessment are.

## How to tell if your hair can take it

Five checks a stylist should do before quoting you. You can do a rough version of the first two yourself:

1. **Stretch test.** Take one wet strand and stretch it gently. Healthy hair stretches and springs back. Hair that snaps immediately or stays stretched is compromised.
2. **Porosity check.** Drop a clean strand in a glass of water. If it sinks quickly, your hair is highly porous — it will absorb treatment fast and may need repair first.
3. **Breakage inspection.** Look at the ends in strong light. Split ends, white dots and uneven lengths indicate existing damage.
4. **Chemical history.** Count your bleach, colour and straightening services in the last 12 months. Two or more bleach sessions in three months is a red flag.
5. **Scalp check.** Any active irritation, flaking, reaction history or recent scalp treatment needs to be resolved before a chemical service. If something looks like a medical issue, see a doctor first — a salon can't and shouldn't diagnose it.

## If you've already had a bad keratin elsewhere

This is more common than most salons admit, and it's usually recoverable.

**First, stop adding treatment.** No more chemical processing until the condition is assessed — including a repeat keratin.

**Second, get an honest diagnosis.** Bring your hair in and ask what state it's actually in. The useful answer describes porosity, breakage and where the damage sits, not just "it's damaged".

**Third, expect repair before styling.** Usually that means hair botox or a protein/moisture rebuild, spread across 2–3 sittings over 6–8 weeks. It's slower and less dramatic than a keratin, and it's the only route that doesn't risk making it worse.

**Fourth, expect the result to fade rather than fail.** A bad keratin doesn't usually disappear overnight — it coats, then gradually fades unevenly, leaving patchy texture. Trimming the worst ends usually helps more than another treatment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does keratin damage hair permanently?**
No. Keratin doesn't change your hair's internal structure, and hair returns gradually to its natural texture as the coating fades. Serious breakage from incorrect application can take months to grow out, which is different from permanent damage.

**Is keratin safe for coloured or bleached hair?**
It depends on how much bleaching the hair has had and when. Recent, repeated bleaching is the main contraindication. That's a decision made after a strand assessment, not over the phone.

**Is keratin safe during pregnancy?**
Tell your stylist if you're pregnant. Different formulas have different considerations, and some salons prefer to postpone or use specific products. Ask what formula they use and discuss it with your doctor if you have concerns. `[NEEDS CLIENT INPUT: studio's stated policy on pregnancy]`

**Can keratin cause hair loss?**
Keratin doesn't affect the hair follicle, so it doesn't cause hair loss. Excessive tension or harsh handling during any styling can contribute to breakage and traction issues, which is why technique matters.

**How often can I get keratin done?**
Professional guidance generally suggests no more than about three times a year, depending on your hair. Repeated stacking is one of the avoidable causes of damage.

**What if my hair is already damaged?**
Repair first. Hair botox or a protein treatment, then reassess. Trying to smooth damaged hair usually makes the damage more visible. [See how we decide →](/blog/keratin-vs-smoothening-vs-hair-botox)
