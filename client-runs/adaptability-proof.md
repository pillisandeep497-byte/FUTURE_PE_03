# Adaptability Proof — One SEO Cluster System, Six Business Types

The task requires a "reusable prompt structure for multiple clients". This proves it: same prompts (`00` → `06`), only the discovery brief swapped. Six different industries, six genuinely different keyword strategies, cluster shapes and content angles.

**Read the cluster shapes side by side. If two industries produced the same structure, the system has failed.** They don't.

---

## 1. Hair & bridal studio — LOCAL SERVICE, HIGH-TICKET TREATMENT
**Business:** Glow Studio by Sanjana, MVP Colony, Visakhapatnam · **Full build-out**
**Pillar:** Keratin Treatment in Visakhapatnam: Cost, Process, Results & Honest Advice
**Cluster shape:** Comparison · Aftercare/how-to · Objection ("will it damage my hair") · Cost · Local
**Winning angle:** publish real prices and admit when you refuse a booking. Competitors do neither.
**Conversion path:** blog → WhatsApp photo → price in 10 minutes
→ Full pack: `client-runs/01-glow-studio-vizag/`

## 2. Dental clinic — YMYL, TRUST-LED, COMPLIANCE-HEAVY

**Business:** Sri Sai Dental Care, MVP Colony, Visakhapatnam *(representative)*
**Pillar:** Dental Implants in Visakhapatnam: Cost, Procedure, Recovery & Risks
**Cluster shape:** Cost breakdown · Procedure walkthrough (how-to) · Objection ("is implant surgery painful/safe") · Comparison (implant vs bridge vs denture) · Local (choosing a dentist in [area])
**Keyword strategy shift:** the cost query is the single biggest driver — and clinics hide prices. Publishing a *range with the variables* (bone graft needed? single vs multiple? brand of implant?) wins the query and pre-qualifies the patient.
**Content shifts:**
- **YMYL compliance**: every claim needs a source; recovery timelines must be hedged; "results vary" is mandatory; no before/after implying guaranteed outcomes.
- **Objection articles carry more weight** — fear of pain and cost drives more searches than the procedure itself.
- **Author E-E-A-T is non-negotiable**: named dentist, registration number, years practising, hospital affiliations.
- Comparison articles must be genuinely neutral — recommending an implant when a bridge suits the patient destroys trust in a medical category.
**Cluster size:** 1 pillar + 6 supports over 4 months (medical content needs slower, more careful review).

## 3. Coaching institute — PARENT-FACING, RESULTS-LED, SEASONAL

**Business:** Vidya Academy — IIT-JEE & EAMCET, Visakhapatnam *(representative)*
**Pillar:** EAMCET Coaching in Visakhapatnam: Fees, Batch Sizes, Results & How to Choose
**Cluster shape:** Fees/cost · Comparison (offline vs online vs hybrid coaching) · Objection ("is coaching necessary if I'm self-studying") · Results/how-to (rank improvement plan) · Local (choosing an institute in [area]) · Parent-focused (how to evaluate a coaching centre)
**Keyword strategy shift:** two audiences search the same topic with different words — **students** ("how to prepare for EAMCET in 6 months", "best books") and **parents** ("coaching fees Visakhapatnam", "is coaching worth it"). The pillar targets the *parent's* commercial query; supports capture the student's informational queries and link up.
**Content shifts:**
- **Results claims must be verifiable** — "48 students per batch" is fine; "guaranteed rank" is not, and unverifiable rank claims are a legal risk.
- **Seasonality drives the calendar**: publish September–December so articles mature before the admission window; update result claims each June.
- The "who this isn't for" section is unusually powerful here ("don't join if you can't attend six days a week").
**Cluster size:** 1 pillar + 5 supports, published ahead of the admission season.

## 4. Digital marketing agency — B2B, SHORT SALES CYCLE, HIGH COMPETITION

**Business:** a 4-person agency in Visakhapatnam serving local businesses *(representative)*
**Pillar:** Local SEO for Small Businesses in Visakhapatnam: What It Costs and What Actually Works
**Cluster shape:** Cost/pricing models · Comparison (SEO vs Google Ads vs social) · Objection ("how long before I see results", "is SEO dead") · Process (how we run a 90-day SEO sprint) · Industry-specific ("SEO for salons/clinics/restaurants")
**Keyword strategy shift:** B2B buyers search *process and price* questions ("how much does local seo cost in india", "how long does seo take to work") far more than service queries. The money keywords are long-tail and comparison-led.
**Content shifts:**
- **Information gain comes from proprietary process** — publish your actual 90-day sprint, with the checklists, because competitors won't.
- **Case studies with real numbers** replace testimonials ("clinic X: 340 impressions → 4,200 in 90 days") — always with client permission.
- Comparison articles must engage honestly with competitors, including when Google Ads beats SEO for a given business.
- Higher publishing capacity (agencies write faster), so 1 pillar + 7 supports is realistic.
**Cluster size:** 1 pillar + 7 supports + a monthly "what changed in local search" series.

## 5. SaaS tool — PRODUCT-LED, HIGH-INTENT, FEATURE-SATURATED

**Business:** a GST invoicing tool for Indian freelancers *(representative)*
**Pillar:** GST Invoicing for Freelancers in India: The Complete Setup Guide
**Cluster shape:** Comparison (vs Excel vs vs other tools) · How-to (raise a GST invoice correctly) · Objection ("do freelancers need GST registration") · Cost (GST filing costs explained) · Problem-specific ("client won't pay, what's the legal route")
**Keyword strategy shift:** SaaS queries sit on **compliance and process questions**, not features. The highest-intent content answers regulatory questions that change with every budget cycle — and those pages need quarterly updates, which is itself a ranking advantage.
**Content shifts:**
- **Templates and calculators beat prose** — a downloadable GST invoice template earns links and emails, which no article can.
- **Update cadence is the strategy**: tax rules change yearly; pages that stay current outrank pages that were merely right once.
- Bottom-of-funnel comparison content is where the trials come from; the pillar is for topical authority.
- Zero "benefits of automation" filler — the reader searches because they have a filing deadline.
**Cluster size:** 1 pillar + 5 supports + 2 tool pages, updated quarterly.

## 6. Diagnostic centre — HYPER-LOCAL, VOLUME-SEARCH, HEALTH COMPLIANCE

**Business:** A NABL-accredited diagnostics lab in Gajuwaka, Visakhapatnam *(representative)*
**Pillar:** Full Body Health Checkups in Visakhapatnam: What to Test, When, and What It Costs
**Cluster shape:** Cost (test-by-test price guide) · How-to (how to prepare for a fasting blood test) · Objection ("are home sample collections reliable/safe") · Comparison (which checkup package fits whom) · Local (home collection in [area] + test turnaround)
**Keyword strategy shift:** demand is dominated by **"test name + price"** and **"test name + near me"** — both transactional, both underserved. Publishing a real, structured price list as text (not a PDF image) is the single highest-value action, and it also captures the AI-answer citations for "how much does X cost".
**Content shifts:**
- **Compliance is the constraint**: no diagnosis or interpretation on the website (that's the doctor's role), no claims about detecting disease, accreditation named exactly, and a medical reviewer credited.
- **Location pages ARE justified here** — unlike most industries — because a lab genuinely has branches with different test availability. Distinct branch pages with real addresses, timings and test menus pass the doorway test; templated ones don't.
- Turnaround times are the strongest trust signal and the most-searched unknown ("when will I get my report?").
**Cluster size:** 1 pillar + 6 supports + genuine branch pages (3–5, each with distinct content).

## Side-by-side: what actually changes

| Dimension | Salon | Dental clinic | Coaching | Agency | SaaS | Diagnostics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pillar intent | Commercial-investigation | Commercial | Commercial (parent) | Commercial (process) | Informational-compliance | Commercial + local |
| Cluster shape | cmp·how · **obj** · cost · local | cost · **how** · **obj** · cmp · local | cost · cmp · **obj** · results · parent | cost · cmp · **obj** · process · industry | cmp · how · **obj** · cost · problem | cost · how · **obj** · cmp · local(+branch) |
| Price transparency | High value, rarely done | High value, rarely done | High value | Publish models, not numbers | Free tier + per-seat | **Highest value in the set** |
| Compliance load | Medium | **Very high (YMYL)** | Medium (results claims) | Low | Medium (tax accuracy) | **Very high (medical)** |
| Author requirement | Real stylist | **Registered dentist** | Named faculty | Agency founder | Product/CA reviewer | **Medical reviewer** |
| Publishing capacity | 2/month | 1–2/month | 2/month | 6–8/month | 4/month | 3/month |
| Location pages justified? | No (1 article) | No | No | No | No | **Yes — real branches only** |
| Content freshness | Quarterly prices | Yearly guidelines | Yearly results | Monthly | Quarterly (tax rules) | Yearly test menu |
| Never sounds like | A chain salon | A hospital brochure | A coaching ad | A guru thread | A feature list | A fear-based clinic |

**What stays constant across all six:** discovery-first briefing, free keyword research, one primary keyword per page, pillar + supports with 2-up links, answer-first openings, an honest-limitation section, the information-gain requirement, the 30-point scorecard, and the scaled-content-safety rule (publish only what you can review).

That's the system. Everything above is the variable.

## Reproduce any of these in ~3 hours

1. `prompts/00` with the owner, plus 45 minutes of free keyword research
2. `prompts/01` → keyword map + cluster topology + cannibalisation check
3. `prompts/02` → outline · `prompts/03` → pillar · `prompts/05` → local adaptation
4. `prompts/04` × 5 → supporting articles (one run each, never batched)
5. `prompts/06` → audit, fix every High, check the 2026 policy gate

One system, unlimited clients. At ₹8,000–₹15,000 per cluster, that's a real service business — and it compounds for the client, which is what keeps them paying monthly.
